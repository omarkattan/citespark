The problem

Ranking first is no use if the answer never mentions you.

A growing share of buying research now happens inside a chat window. The model reads a handful of sources, writes one answer, and names three or four companies. There is no page two. You are either in that answer or you are invisible, and nothing in Google Search Console will tell you which.

Failure one

Never named

The engine answers the question and lists your competitors. You are not in the candidate set it read from.

Failure two

Named, never cited

It mentions you but sources the detail elsewhere. No click, no control of how you are described.

Failure three

Named last

You appear fourth in a list nobody reads to the end. Position is earned through corroboration, not copy.

FAQs

Questions worth asking before you trust any of this.

What exactly is CiteSpark measuring?

Whether AI assistants name your brand when someone asks a buying question in your category, and where you appear when they do. We ask each tracked question multiple times per engine and report the percentage of answers you appeared in, along with your average position in the list and which domains the engine cited to build the answer.

Which engines do you track?

ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Perplexity and Claude, with Google AI Overviews alongside. You choose which to run, because each one you add multiplies the cost of a cycle. Three is usually the right balance between coverage and spend.

Why percentages instead of a rank?

Because AI answers are not deterministic. Ask the same question twice and you get different answers, sometimes with different companies in them. Any tool that hands you a single position number is reporting one roll of the dice as though it were a ranking.

CiteSpark asks each question several times per cycle and reports how often you appeared. On the questions screen you can see each individual run as a tick mark, so the variance is visible rather than hidden behind an average.

How is this different from rank tracking?

Rank tracking tells you where a page sits in a list of ten blue links. There is no list here. There is one answer, it names three or four companies, and there is no page two to be on. The mechanics of getting into that answer are also different: corroboration across independent sources matters more than on-page optimisation, and being cited as a source is separate from being named as a recommendation.

Does it tell me what to do, or just what is wrong?

It tells you what to do. Every finding becomes an action with a named target, ranked by impact over effort, with the evidence attached so you can check the reasoning. Content gaps, citable assets, entity and schema fixes, comparison pages, third-party sources to get listed on, engine-specific crawler problems, and pages that already convert AI traffic and are worth copying.

You can mark actions as started, done or dismissed, and the status survives the next measurement cycle.

What are fan-out queries and why do they matter?

They are the searches an engine runs before writing its answer. Ask ChatGPT which clinic to use for clear aligners in Dubai and it may search "orthodontic clinics Dubai clear aligners cost" first, then write from what it finds.

That is an ordinary Google query. If you do not rank for it, you were never a candidate, and rewriting your page for the conversational question will not help. It is the most directly actionable signal in the whole dataset, and we surface every one.

How often does it run?

Weekly by default, which suits the pace at which this actually moves. Daily is available and costs roughly seven times as much, so it is worth it for a handful of high-stakes questions rather than an entire set. You can also trigger a cycle by hand whenever you have shipped a change and want to see whether it landed.

Can I track competitors?

Yes, and you should. Share of voice against named rivals is often the number that gets a budget approved. When a competitor consistently beats you, CiteSpark rolls it into a single comparison action rather than repeating itself for every question they win.

Does it connect to Google Analytics?

Yes. GA4 added a native AI Assistant channel in 2026, and we read it, but we also run our own classification from session source. That matters because the native channel is not retroactive, so on its own your history starts mid-2026. Our version reaches back across your existing data.

Some AI referrals arrive with no referrer at all and land in Direct, so both numbers are a floor rather than a total. We say so in the interface rather than quietly overstating the figure.

What is an answer check, and why price on them?

One answer check is one question put to one engine, once. Twenty-five questions across two engines with three runs each is 150 checks per cycle, or roughly 650 a month at weekly cadence.

We price this way because it is what the product actually costs us. Every answer requires the engine to run a live web search, and that search carries a real fee. Pretending otherwise would mean either quietly capping you or losing money on heavy users. Your usage is visible in the app at all times, and a cycle trims itself to fit rather than running you over a limit.

Can I change or cancel my plan?

Any time, from the billing screen inside the app. Upgrades apply immediately and are prorated. Cancellations run to the end of the period you have paid for, then drop to the Free plan with your history intact. No contract, no notice period, and nobody to email.
